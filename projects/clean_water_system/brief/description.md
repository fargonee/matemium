# How a City Gets Clean Water

## Project identity

- **Subject:** General Education
- **Project slug:** `clean_water_system`
- **Status:** First authored prototype complete; engine feedback recorded; flagship render not yet accepted
- **Central question:** What happens between a natural water source and safe water arriving at a tap?
- **Primary audience:** General learners, families, and classroom audiences
- **Target format:** 16:9 flagship master, approximately 2–3 minutes

## Purpose

This project should demonstrate how Matemium explains an everyday system spanning geography, infrastructure, physical processes, measurement, and public responsibility.

It must make the treatment chain understandable while acknowledging that real systems vary by city and source. The project is educational, not operational water-safety advice.

## Learning outcomes

The viewer should understand:

- source water contains different kinds of suspended and dissolved material;
- treatment commonly uses multiple barriers rather than one magical filter;
- coagulation/flocculation, settling, filtration, and disinfection serve different purposes;
- storage, pressure, distribution, and monitoring remain part of the system;
- wastewater treatment is a separate downstream system;
- local treatment processes and water-quality rules differ.

## Narrative arc

1. **Begin at the tap:** Follow a glass of water backward through a building and city pipe.
2. **Reveal the network:** Zoom out to storage, pumping, treatment plant, and source.
3. **Raw-water challenge:** Introduce representative particles and microorganisms without sensationalism.
4. **Coagulation and flocculation:** Show small suspended particles gathering into larger removable clusters.
5. **Settling and filtration:** Follow distinct material paths through both stages.
6. **Disinfection:** Explain the purpose and controlled nature of the final barrier.
7. **Distribution:** Trace pressure, storage, monitoring points, and household delivery.
8. **A disturbance:** Show how a sensor reading or pipe problem creates an operational response.
9. **System boundary:** Distinguish drinking-water treatment from wastewater treatment.
10. **Synthesis:** Return to the glass and reveal the coordinated system behind an ordinary action.

## Visual and motion direction

- Use a continuous source-to-tap spatial path as the project’s organizing structure.
- Use scale changes to move from watershed to facility to particles.
- Keep treated-water flow direction persistent and easy to follow.
- Distinguish physical removal, chemical treatment, monitoring, and distribution.
- Use schematic infrastructure rather than falsely precise plant engineering.
- Avoid implying that clear-looking water is necessarily safe.

## Matemium capabilities this project must demonstrate

- large spatial systems and process diagrams;
- multiscale transitions;
- particle and flow animation;
- staged transformations;
- infrastructure networks and monitoring feedback;
- overview-to-detail camera movement;
- reusable components for tanks, pipes, sensors, and process stages.

## Required source and assets

- Build the primary system procedurally.
- Base the stage order on a documented conventional surface-water treatment process.
- Keep process variations and regional limitations in project notes.
- No external photographs or video are required.

## Scope boundaries

- Do not provide instructions for making unsafe water potable at home.
- Do not claim that every city uses the exact depicted process.
- Do not represent disinfection as removing every possible contaminant.
- Do not merge drinking-water and wastewater systems.
- Avoid unexplained microscopic imagery presented as literal scale.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every stage has a distinct purpose and visible input/output.
- Flow remains traceable across the entire system.
- At least one monitoring-and-response loop is shown.
- Scale changes are labeled and do not imply false physical proportions.
- Scientific and public-health claims receive review before publication.
- The project renders offline and demonstrates broad system-explanation capability.
