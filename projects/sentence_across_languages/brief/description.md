# How One Thought Changes Across Languages

## Project identity

- **Subject:** Language Learning
- **Project slug:** `sentence_across_languages`
- **Status:** First authored prototype complete; engine feedback recorded; flagship render not yet accepted
- **Central question:** How can different languages organize the same intended meaning in different ways?
- **Primary audience:** Curious language learners; the exact language pair or trio must be chosen with qualified review
- **Target format:** 16:9 flagship master, approximately 2–3 minutes, with a later portrait-friendly sequence

## Purpose

This project must show language as a structured transformation of meaning, not word-for-word substitution. A single concrete thought should pass through semantic roles, sentence structure, morphology, word order, and timed pronunciation.

The final language selection should favor genuinely instructive contrasts and available expert review. English plus Uzbek and one additional language is a promising direction, but it is not fixed by this brief.

## Learning outcomes

The viewer should understand:

- languages can encode the same roles with different word orders;
- endings, particles, agreement, or position may carry grammatical information;
- literal word alignment is not always a valid translation;
- sentence structure and information emphasis can differ;
- pronunciation unfolds as a timed sequence rather than a static spelling;
- variation is systematic, not a sign that one language is more logical.

## Narrative arc

1. **Establish meaning:** Show a simple event with actor, action, object, time, and location.
2. **Build semantic roles:** Label the meaning before introducing any particular language.
3. **First language:** Arrange words and grammatical markers into a natural sentence.
4. **Transform:** Move the same semantic-role objects into the structure of the second language.
5. **Expose morphology:** Expand a meaningful ending, particle, or agreement marker and show what information it carries.
6. **Contrast emphasis:** Compare a neutral sentence with one controlled transformation.
7. **Pronunciation pass:** Highlight syllables or phoneme groups in synchronization with reviewed audio.
8. **Meaning check:** Return both sentences to the original event and show that structure changed while the core intended meaning remained.
9. **Synthesis:** Frame grammar as a set of choices for packaging meaning.

## Visual and motion direction

- Give semantic roles stable identities independent of their word forms.
- Animate reordering spatially rather than replacing an entire sentence abruptly.
- Separate orthography, morphology, syntax, and pronunciation into distinct layers.
- Use glosses carefully and never imply perfect one-to-one word correspondence.
- Highlight pronunciation timing without caricaturing accent.
- Support scripts beyond Latin if the selected languages require them.

## Matemium capabilities this project must demonstrate

- token and phrase transformations;
- sentence-tree or dependency relationships;
- spatial reordering;
- character- and morpheme-level highlighting;
- timed pronunciation synchronization;
- multilingual typography;
- reusable structured linguistic data.

## Required source and assets

- Select the final sentence and languages only after native or qualified review is available.
- Store tokens, roles, glosses, morphology, and timing as explicit data.
- Record or synthesize pronunciation only with correct speaker/language settings and review.
- The source-only app example must remain useful when audio is absent; audio is an optional generated or downloaded asset.

## Scope boundaries

- Do not present machine-like literal translation as natural speech.
- Do not generalize one sentence pattern to an entire language.
- Do not label normal grammatical difference as strange or inefficient.
- Do not ship unreviewed pronunciation.
- Avoid a sentence so complex that the core structural contrast disappears.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every translation, gloss, grammatical label, and pronunciation receives qualified review.
- The semantic-role layer remains stable across transformations.
- At least one word-order contrast and one morphology contrast are made explicit.
- The project remains understandable without sound and becomes richer with sound.
- Text is readable in every selected script and no glyph fallback occurs.
- The result demonstrates Matemium’s ability to teach transformations and timed symbolic sequences.
