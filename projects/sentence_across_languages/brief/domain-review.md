# Domain review — Sentence across languages

**Review date:** 2026-07-30  
**Reviewer:** AI source-and-structure review; standard-Uzbek native-speaker
review remains required before public language sign-off.

## Claims checked

1. English commonly uses subject–verb–object order in a neutral declarative
   clause.
2. Uzbek is head-final and has a neutral subject–object–verb pattern.
3. Uzbek `-ni` marks an accusative/definite direct object in this example.
4. Standard Uzbek `-yap` forms a present progressive, and third person uses
   the `-ti` form in `o‘qiyapti`.
5. Case marking permits more ordering flexibility than a rigid four-token
   formula would suggest.

## Evidence

- Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, *Object Incorporation in Uzbek*,
  head-final SOV order and Uzbek case inventory:
  https://semanticsarchive.net/Archive/zBhZTI0M/Object%20Incorporation%20in%20Uzbek.pdf
- ERIC, *Uzbek: Language Competencies for Peace Corps Volunteers in
  Uzbekistan*, pedagogical language and grammar resource:
  https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED353829
- Turkolog, *Uzbek Present Progressive*, `-yap` progressive and third-person
  `-ti`, including a book-reading example:
  https://www.turkolog.ist/uzbek/Uzbek_present_progressive.pdf

## Deterministic checks

- Both records contain the same four semantic role keys.
- English order is actor, action, object, time.
- Uzbek order is actor, time, object, action.
- The surface sentence matches its four stored tokens.
- Every reading-path target has a stable semantic identifier.

## Assumptions and simplifications

- The sentence is a controlled standard-language teaching example.
- Role labels simplify the distinction between semantic roles and syntactic
  relations.
- Morpheme glosses are selective; they are not a full interlinear analysis.
- No pronunciation or dialect claim is made by the silent reading path.
