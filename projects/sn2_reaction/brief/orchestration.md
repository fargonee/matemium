# Orchestration — SN2 Reaction

## Format and visual system

- Portrait 9:16 master designed to prove that bundled examples are not confined
  to landscape lectures.
- Oxygen/nucleophile is coral; bromine/leaving identity is violet.
- Forming interactions are mint; breaking interactions are orange.
- Persistent `R¹`, `R²`, and `R³` colors make inversion inspectable.
- A gold energy curve provides a separate but registered representation.

## Beat choreography

### C00 — Hook

Start with the two-bond paradox and a compact generic equation. State the
concerted claim before introducing detailed geometry.

### C01 — Reactant roles

Create one semantic diagram. Highlight the nucleophile node and C—Br edge to
establish the two active regions. Avoid decorative molecular rotation that a
2D projection cannot support honestly.

### C02 — Concerted passage

Morph the registered diagram to a transition-state target containing two
partial bonds, then morph to the inverted product projection. Keep the
registered diagram in the root tape: an overlay-focus experiment duplicated
the molecule and was removed during visual repair. The generic substituent
identities remain stable.

### C03 — Energy view

Create one generic plot and morph its registered marker through reactants,
transition state, and products. Emphasize the maximum semantically. This is a
staged correspondence with the preceding molecular states, not a claim of
continuous shared-clock synchronization.

### C04 — Sterics

Create an accessible backside approach diagram, then morph it into a crowded
variant using the same node-edge language. The comparison changes a general
scenario parameter rather than invoking a chemistry-specific engine primitive.

### C05 — Finale

Resolve the story as four observations of one event and isolate the compact
transition-state signature.

## Implementation notes

- Molecular and energy data are deterministic and generated locally.
- `Diagram`, `DataPlot`, `ElementMorph`, semantic state transitions, and camera
  focus cover the production without engine changes. Focus is reserved for the
  final isolated signature rather than used on the dense molecular panel.
- The molecular geometry is a documented 2D teaching projection. It is not an
  optimized 3D structure or a molecular-dynamics simulation.
