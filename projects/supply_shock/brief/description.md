# How a Supply Shock Moves Through a Market

## Project identity

- **Subject:** Economics
- **Project slug:** `supply_shock`
- **Status:** Flagship preview accepted; final 1920×1080 master and independent economics sign-off pending
- **Central question:** How can one disruption change prices, quantities, business decisions, and household behavior?
- **Primary audience:** General learners and introductory economics students
- **Target format:** 16:9 mute flagship master, approximately 45–60 seconds

## Purpose

This project should explain a negative supply shock through a concrete fictional market, such as wheat or coffee. It must connect a causal event to a supply-demand model while keeping the distinction between a model and reality explicit.

The goal is not to advocate a policy. It is to demonstrate disciplined causal reasoning, alternative responses, and model limitations.

## Learning outcomes

The viewer should understand:

- what a supply curve represents;
- why reduced productive capacity shifts supply rather than merely moving along it;
- how equilibrium price and quantity change in the basic model;
- how producers and consumers respond over time;
- how substitution, inventories, expectations, and policy can alter the path;
- why a diagram is a simplified conditional model, not a prediction machine.

## Narrative arc

1. **A functioning market:** Establish ordinary flows among producers, sellers, and households.
2. **The shock:** Introduce a clearly defined harvest failure or input disruption.
3. **Causal chain:** Trace lower available supply into inventories and offered quantities.
4. **Model view:** Shift the supply curve while holding demand initially fixed.
5. **New equilibrium:** Animate price and quantity changes with labels tied to the real-world flow.
6. **Behavior over time:** Show substitution, delayed production response, and inventory release.
7. **Scenario comparison:** Contrast quick recovery, persistent disruption, and demand reduction.
8. **Model boundary:** Identify assumptions and variables intentionally omitted.
9. **Synthesis:** Return from the graph to affected participants and summarize the causal sequence.

## Visual and motion direction

- Use one consistent visual identity for supply, demand, price, and quantity.
- Keep curve shifts distinct from movement along a curve.
- Coordinate the market-flow diagram, graph, and time-series view.
- Label assumptions near the relevant model rather than in a final disclaimer wall.
- Human figures should represent roles, not stereotypes or emotional manipulation.
- Uncertainty should appear as scenarios or ranges rather than false numerical precision.

## Matemium capabilities this project must demonstrate

- economic graphs with controlled curve shifts;
- causal networks and flow diagrams;
- scenario branching;
- time-series changes;
- synchronized model and real-world representations;
- annotated assumptions and counterfactual comparisons;
- structured data driving several views.

## Required source and assets

- Use fictional or clearly illustrative values unless a reviewed dataset is later selected.
- Store scenario assumptions and curve parameters in transparent configuration.
- Build all graphs and diagrams procedurally.
- No live market data or network access may be required.

## Scope boundaries

- Do not present equilibrium as instantaneous real-world adjustment.
- Do not conflate inflation across an economy with one product’s relative price change.
- Do not infer moral or political conclusions from the simplified model.
- Do not use real current events without sourcing and review.
- Avoid unnecessary calculus in the primary explanation.

## Acceptance criteria

- Curve shifts and movements along curves are visually and verbally correct.
- Each causal link is explicit and no major conclusion appears from nowhere.
- At least three plausible adjustment scenarios are distinguishable.
- Assumptions and limitations are visible within the narrative.
- The project renders offline and supports parameter changes from structured source.
- The result demonstrates Matemium’s capacity for causal and model-based explanation.
