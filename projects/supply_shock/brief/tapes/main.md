# Visible content — Supply Shock

## C00 — Physical disruption

- A fictional grain market begins at `P=60`, `Q=40`.
- Harvest failure reduces availability, shifts supply, changes equilibrium,
  and prompts households and producers to respond.

## C01 — Baseline model

- Demand: `P=100−Q`.
- Supply: `P=Q+20`.
- Their intersection is `E₀=(Q=40, P=60)`.

## C02 — Supply shift

- Productive-capacity loss changes supply to `P=Q+40`.
- Less is offered at every price; the new equilibrium is
  `E₁=(Q=30, P=70)`.
- Explicitly distinguish shifting the curve from selecting a point on it.

## C03 — Adjustment paths

- Compare quick recovery, persistent disruption, and demand adaptation on
  identical axes.
- Show the baseline, immediate shock, and adapting-demand snapshots.

## C04 — Model boundary

- Demand is initially fixed; the product and quantities are fictional.
- Equilibrium is not instantaneous.
- Inventories, policy, and expectations are omitted.
- A relative price change is not automatically economy-wide inflation.

## C05 — Synthesis

- Capacity falls → supply shifts → modeled price rises and quantity falls →
  choices adapt.
- Changing assumptions may change the path; causal accounting must remain
  visible.
