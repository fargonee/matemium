# The Chain Reaction That Began World War I

## Project identity

- **Subject:** History
- **Project slug:** `wwi_chain_reaction`
- **Status:** Full-tape authoring proof accepted; final mute-video master and independent historian review pending
- **Central question:** How did a regional assassination become a European war within weeks?
- **Primary audience:** General history learners and secondary-school students
- **Target format:** 16:9 flagship master, approximately 3 minutes

## Purpose

This project must explain escalation as a sequence of decisions made within alliances, military plans, fears, and incomplete information. It should resist the misleading image of alliances as an automatic row of dominoes.

The project must distinguish chronology, geography, relationships, and causality. It should also clearly separate established events from interpretive claims.

## Learning outcomes

The viewer should understand:

- the assassination was a trigger within existing tensions, not a complete explanation;
- the July Crisis unfolded through specific decisions and deadlines;
- alliances constrained choices but did not mechanically force every action;
- mobilization plans changed the perceived cost of delay;
- geography shaped the widening conflict;
- historical causation can have multiple levels and contested emphasis.

## Narrative arc

1. **Before the trigger:** Establish the central question and distinguish trigger, structures, constraints, and decisions.
2. **Sarajevo:** Locate and date the assassination without dramatized reconstruction.
3. **The July Crisis:** Build a dated sequence of ultimatum, responses, assurances, and mobilizations.
4. **Decision network:** Connect actors to the information and pressures visible at each step.
5. **Geographic expansion:** Move from diplomatic network to map as declarations and military plans widen the conflict.
6. **Slow the “dominoes”:** Revisit two or three moments where leaders still had alternatives.
7. **Layers of cause:** Arrange trigger, structural pressures, plans, and decisions without declaring a single total cause.
8. **Synthesis:** Show the final transition from a Balkan crisis to multi-front European war.

## Visual and motion direction

- Use a clearly labelled schematic of relative geography; do not imply that it
  is a historically precise border map.
- Maintain one visual identity per state across map, timeline, and relationship network.
- Every dated event should occupy a clear chronological position.
- Show messages and decisions as directed connections, not omniscient information.
- Use branching or paused alternatives sparingly to communicate contingency.
- Avoid flags, explosions, or military spectacle as decorative transitions.

## Matemium capabilities this project must demonstrate

- spatial geographic schematics;
- precise timelines;
- relationship and decision networks;
- causal layers and branching possibilities;
- synchronized geographic and chronological views;
- evidence labels and distinctions between event and interpretation;
- data-driven events that can be reviewed independently of layout code.

## Required source and assets

- Create an explicit event dataset containing date, actors, action, location, and source note.
- Use public-domain or original simplified map geometry with provenance documented in source comments.
- Maintain a research note or source list when authoring begins.
- The bundled runtime project must remain usable offline.

## Scope boundaries

- Do not claim that alliances alone caused the war.
- Do not represent disputed interpretations as uncontested facts.
- Do not compress dates in a way that changes event order.
- Do not use modern borders on a 1914 map without explanation.
- Do not attempt to summarize the entire war; end when the escalation question is answered.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every date, border, declaration, and actor relationship receives historical review.
- Timeline and map states never contradict each other.
- The narrative identifies both structural conditions and human decisions.
- At least two points of contingency are explained without speculative alternate-history storytelling.
- Sources and interpretive boundaries are visible in project documentation.
- The result proves Matemium can explain time, geography, relationships, and cause together.
