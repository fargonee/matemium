# Domain review — World War I chain reaction

**Review date:** 2026-07-30  
**Reviewer:** AI source-and-chronology review; independent historian review
remains required before public domain sign-off.

## Claims checked

1. Franz Ferdinand and Sophie were assassinated in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914.
2. German backing on 5 July preceded Austria-Hungary’s 23 July ultimatum.
3. Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on 28 July.
4. Russian general mobilization on 30 July preceded Germany’s declarations of
   war on Russia and France on 1 and 3 August.
5. Germany invaded Belgium and Britain declared war on 4 August.
6. British mediation was proposed during the crisis, so escalation should not
   be presented as automatic.

## Evidence

- Imperial War Museums, *How the World Went to War in 1914*, chronology,
  decisions, mobilization, declarations, and interpretive context:
  https://www.iwm.org.uk/history/how-the-world-went-to-war-in-1914
- Imperial War Museums, *What You Need to Know About Pre-First World War
  Alliances*, alliance context and limits of a simple automatic-bloc model:
  https://www.iwm.org.uk/history/what-you-need-to-know-about-pre-first-world-war-alliances

## Deterministic checks

- The source dataset contains nine events in strictly increasing order.
- The first and last entries are 28 June and 4 August.
- Event categories are limited to trigger, decision, mobilization, and war.
- Every network and geography node has a stable semantic identifier.

## Assumptions and simplifications

- The display selects events needed to answer the escalation question; it is
  not a complete diplomatic history of the July Crisis.
- “Thirty-eight days” counts the dated span inclusively.
- Geography shows relative placement only, not borders or scale.
- Causal-layer wording is interpretive scaffolding rather than a declaration
  that historians agree on one hierarchy of causes.
